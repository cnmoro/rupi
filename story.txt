The lighthouse keeper climbed his spiral stairs at dawn,
His boots echoing against the rusted iron rungs.
Outside, the sea whispered secrets to the stone walls,
The kind only someone who has waited long enough
Might begin to understand. He lit the great lamp,
Watching its beam carve a golden path through fog.
A ship appeared on the horizon, tiny as a toy,
Its whistle a lonely question in the morning air.
He answered with a flash, steady and sure,
As he had done for thirty-seven years.
The vessel turned toward the harbor, safe at last.
He descended the stairs, the routine complete.
On the windowsill sat a letter he hadn't opened—
The only thing in the world that made him hesitate.
He poured coffee, black as the midnight sea,
And sat by the window to watch the waves break.
The lighthouse beam swept over the water again,
An endless conversation between light and dark.
He finally picked up the letter with trembling hands,
And smiled, because he already knew what it said.
